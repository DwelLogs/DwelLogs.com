Sharing a place

Everybody on a place has their own sign-in. What they can see and do comes from two things: the kind of access you gave them, and whose each building is.

The kinds of access

You choose one when you invite somebody, from Invite somebody on your screen. They sign in with their own email and type the code you send them.

On the inviteWhat they can do
The ownerEverything: invite people, hand a building to somebody, remove the place. That is you, on a place you set up.
Change schedules tooSee what is due, log it done, add jobs, and change how often check-ups come round.
See what is due, and log it doneSee the jobs and check-ups, mark them done, and say I will do it on a job.
Look, and nothing elseSee it all, change nothing.

Everything, or one building? An invite can be for the whole place or for one building. One building means they see that building and what is attached to it, and nothing else of yours.

Good until is the last day the code works. Once they have joined, they stay on the place until they leave it from their own screen.

Coming: taking somebody off a place yourself.

Whose a building is

A building on your place can be somebody else’s: a tiny house parked on your land, or a shed you built for someone to use. Whose it is and who can see inside it are separate questions.

What you see of somebody else’s building

It is their call, made when they bring their home in:

  • That it is there, and what it is hooked up to. You see the building, and which of your things it runs on: your well, your septic. A problem with your well is yours to know about, so that always shows. Their own things, check-ups, jobs and costs do not.
  • Everything in it. It shows like any other building on the place.

If they move on

Each building that is somebody else’s says whether it goes with them or stays here. Something on wheels goes with them; something anchored to the ground stays. You can change it when a building is unusual, like a tiny house on a foundation.

Coming: the step for when somebody actually leaves, which ends their access and sends a building that goes with them on its way with its history.

Who did it, and who paid

When somebody marks a job done as they do it, their name goes on it in the history. When work is entered from memory, it says Us, Whoever was here before or A company, never a person.

When somebody else paid for something, that line says so: Pumped the tank · $350 · paid by Mom.

Neither is ever added up. There are no per-person totals, no counts and no balance. Help that never comes with a receipt counts just as much, and a tally would only ever see the money.

Set-ups

Family with their own home on your land Works today

Mom’s tiny house, parked on the ranch and hooked up to its well and septic.

  1. Set your place up first, including the well, septic and anything else her home will run on.
  2. Invite somebody. Under Do they have their own home here? pick Yes, not on here yet — they will bring it.
  3. She signs in and accepts. If she has already set her home up in DwelLogs on her own phone, it offers to bring it in, with its things, check-ups, jobs and history.
  4. She chooses what you see, ticks what her home gets from your place, and says whether her well is the same one as yours. If it is, hers folds into yours rather than becoming a second well. Her own solar, holding tank and battery stay hers.
  5. From then on her app opens on her home, with the whole place one tap away.

Nothing set up on her phone? Add her home to your place as a building, then hand it to her from its page once she has joined.

A building you gave someone Coming

The shed you built for her: hers to use, but anchored, so it stays with the place. Handing a building over from its page is set aside for now while the basics are made solid. Add the shed to your place today; it can be handed over later without starting again.

A home parked for a while Works today

An RV or a tiny house on wheels, staying a season. The same as family with their own home: it goes with them when they leave.

Coming: marking the day they leave, so it stops showing on your place.

Their car Works today

Add it from Add as a vehicle, and under Whose is it? pick them. It is theirs wherever it is parked.

Someone staying and helping out Works today

  1. Invite them with See what is due, and log it done.
  2. They see what needs doing, say I will do it on a job, and mark it done.
  3. When they go, they leave the place from their own screen.

Coming: access that ends on its own on the day they leave, taking somebody off a place yourself, and putting a job out to the whole place for anyone to pick up.

Roommates Partly

Invite each other with Change schedules too, and you can both run the place. Shared chores work the same way: anyone can say I will do it.

Coming: things that are one person’s, like your fridge and their TV, so moving out has a list.

Renting a room Coming

A room that is somebody’s, where you see that it is there and not what is in it, the same way a building can be. Today a building can be somebody’s and a room cannot, so a lodger invited to the place sees the whole house.
